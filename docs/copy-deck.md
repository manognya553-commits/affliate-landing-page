# sBLOOM Affiliate Programme — Affiliate-First Messaging & Copy

Working copy deck · 27 October 2026 · Written from the affiliate’s point of view: homemakers, working people, community connectors and anyone in India whom others listen to and trust

## 1. The shift: talk to the person, not the programme

The current copy explains the programme well. It does not yet answer the questions an affiliate is silently asking. A homemaker in Vijayawada, a bank employee in Hyderabad or a tuition teacher in Guntur does not wonder what a “knowledge-led referral network” is. They wonder:

- Is this for someone like me?
- Is it safe? Will I lose money, or look foolish in front of my family and friends?
- What exactly do I have to do?
- Do I need to know medicine, law or education?
- How will I be paid, and how will I know what happened to my referral?
- Can I do this in Telugu, from my phone, in my free time?

Every section of the page should answer one of these questions in plain words.

One more shift: the word “convince” (or “influence”) sounds like pressure to a first-time affiliate. The page should say people **trust you, ask you and listen to you**. Your job is to **introduce**, never to push.

## 2. Who we are talking to

Start with Telugu-speaking communities in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, but write so any adult in India can see themselves in the page.

- **The homemaker.** Trusted in her family, apartment, school-parents’ group and kitty or bhajan circle. Wants something from home, in her own time, with no investment. Fears being judged or scammed.
- **The working professional.** An employee, teacher or bank or IT worker with colleagues and a long contact list. Wants side income without a second job. Needs low effort and a clear process.
- **The community connector.** Active in an association, temple committee, residents’ welfare group, alumni circle or NRI-family network. Trusted by many. Cares about relationships and clear boundaries.
- **The shop owner or tutor.** Meets many families every week. Needs a simple way to help without disturbing the main business.
- **The retired or senior person.** Wide circle, time and credibility. Needs large text, simple steps and human support.
- **The young adult (18+).** Lives on WhatsApp, Instagram and YouTube. Needs something credible, not a gimmick.

## 3. Messaging principles

1. **Start with what they already do.** People already recommend doctors, schools and advisers. We give that a structure and a reward.
2. **Name the fear, then answer it.** Free? Yes. Expert needed? No. Pressure? None.
3. **Say “introduce”, not “sell”.** The affiliate notices a need, asks permission and makes the introduction. The provider advises.
4. **Be honest about money.** No amounts and no guarantees. But say clearly: every provider sets its own terms, you see them first, and the platform tracks each referral.
5. **Use plain words.** Short sentences, everyday vocabulary, easy to read at school level.
6. **Bilingual by default.** English and Telugu with a visible language toggle. Hindi can follow.
7. **Respect the person.** No “quit your job” or “be your own boss” bravado. A homemaker or a worker should feel respected, not hustled.

### Words to use

introduce · recommend · help someone find · free to join · you’ll see the terms first · track · when it works out · in your own time · people trust you

### Words to avoid

easy money · passive income · guaranteed · get rich · unlimited earnings · hustle · quit your job · downline · recruit · convince · influence · “influencer” as a requirement · any ₹ amount that has not been approved

### What an introduction sounds like

A friendly, permission-first message is all it takes. The affiliate initiates the conversation based on something the person previously mentioned:

- **Healthcare:**
  Neighbour: “You said your mother needs a doctor. I know a service that might help.”
  You (Affiliate): “I can introduce you to a relevant healthcare provider. Would you like me to connect you?”
- **Education:**
  Friend: “You mentioned you’re looking for a coaching institute.”
  You (Affiliate): “I can introduce you to an institute that offers relevant programmes. Would you like to know more?”
- **Professional Services:**
  Business Owner: “I need help improving my business online.”
  You (Affiliate): “I know a service provider who may be able to help. Would you like an introduction?”

The rule behind all three: a clear yes before any details are shared. No income is guaranteed.

## 8. Confirm before publishing

These lines are written to be honest, but each one needs a business decision or a fact check before going live:

- **Payout timing and method**, and whether PAN or bank details are needed at payout. Telling people upfront builds trust.
- **Whether affiliates are ever asked to buy anything or recruit others.** If the answer is no, say so plainly. It is the strongest reassurance for first-time affiliates.
- **Whether there is any minimum activity.** Needed to answer the “I have a job” question precisely.
- **Languages the platform and service briefs support.** Telugu on the page is only a promise if Telugu support exists after joining.
- **Provider verification.** Only mention it if sBLOOM actually reviews providers.
- **Minimum age.** The copy assumes 18+.
- **Response time after the form is submitted.**
- **Service availability by city or state.**
- **A native Telugu review** of every Telugu line, and a final legal review of all earnings wording.
